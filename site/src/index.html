The brief we keep hearing

Capability is not the issue.

We hear the same pattern from HR Directors, Heads of Talent, and business leaders. Capability is not the issue. Translation into senior leadership is.

  1. “We promote experts who struggle as leaders.”
  2. “We are losing high-potential women at VP and Director level.”
  3. “Our leadership programmes are not translating into behaviour change.”
  4. “We have diversity ambitions, but not enough women ready for senior roles.”
  5. “We are not effectively enabling high-potential women to step into leadership.”

Where expertise stops being enough

What got them here will not get them there.

Many senior women are still operating with the mindset that got them promoted ten years ago. Deliver more. Be indispensable. Stay across everything.

But leadership at the next level is not about proving value through output. It is about influence, positioning, and strategic authority.

Until that is understood, no tool sticks. So the work starts there: a genuine mindset shift, then the practical tools to act on it, then the behaviour anchored under real business pressure. The result is women who lead through others, shape decisions, and strengthen the pipeline you depend on.

Why I do this work

I’ve sat on the committees where careers are decided. I know exactly what differentiates the women who step into leadership from the ones who remain valued for delivery.

Twenty years inside Morgan Stanley, Credit Suisse and J.P. Morgan, leading global teams and sitting on the talent and promotion committees, deciding senior progression. The view from that side of the table is unambiguous: brilliance is not what gets you promoted at senior level. Visibility, political intelligence, and the ability to lead through other people is.

I have watched ready, exceptional women get passed over, not because they could not do the job, but because no one had ever shown them how the senior game was actually being played. I know what that costs the individual. I also know what it costs the business: a thin pipeline, repeated promotion misfires, and senior roles filled by default rather than design.

That is the work I do. Turning high-performing women into visible, influential leaders before organisations lose them, overlook them, or underestimate them.
